Write the scene. Storia sets the page.

Type INT. and the line becomes a scene heading. Press Enter for action, Tab for a character, and the dialogue follows the cue. Every element lands at industry margins in 12pt Courier, so the page you print is the page a reader expects.

Log in

In a closed beta, by invitation. Runs in your browser on a laptop, tablet or phone, with nothing to install.

Learn the format by writing it

You don't need to memorise where a cue sits. Enter and Tab move you from one element to the next, the names you've already used come up as you type a character, and Cmd or Ctrl with a number picks any of the ten elements, from shots and lyrics to act breaks. Notes stay on screen for you and never print.

Every draft on its own colour

On a production, each round of changes goes out on the next colour of paper: white, then blue, pink, yellow and on to cherry. Storia gives each new document in a project the next colour, so your third draft never gets mistaken for your first, and the ritual is second nature by the time a production asks it of you.

Plan the story on cards

Each project has a board. Lay the story out card by card and act by act, and drag a card to where it belongs. File cards in groups, give them fields of your own, and link a card to the scenes that play it out: the link opens the draft at that scene.

Bring your script in, take it anywhere

Open a Final Draft (.fdx) or WriterDuet (.wdz) file as a new draft. Print or save a PDF with your title and name on the cover page, or export Fountain, the plain-text format other screenwriting apps open. While you write, Storia saves as you go and keeps count of your pages, scenes and estimated running time.
